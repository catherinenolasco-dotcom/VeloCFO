Case studies

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell Inc. | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

Ridecell had no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount in spreadsheets, and no tracked channel for employee questions, across a company growing into five countries.

What I did

Built a 40+ procedure SOP library, ASKPeople (Jira and Confluence, tiered SLAs), and Sapling as the HR single source of truth integrated with ADP and Zapier connections.

Result

6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, approval cycles cut from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day resolved in 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process that created audit risk, and there was early pushback that a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form requiring documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion New Hire and Offboarding workflows.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

A job search run by AI

Personal project | Sep 2026 - Oct 2026

Challenge

My own application process had 11 manual steps spread across a job tracker, Asana, Notion, a resume, an ATS check, and a Drive folder, and the same busywork repeated for every role.

What I did

Mapped every step, then built a Claude workflow where one job link creates the Asana task from a template, writes the Notion tracker row and links the two, builds a tailored resume from my master resume, runs the Jobscan check, and files the Drive folder and applied status. Submitting the application and sending LinkedIn messages stay manual on purpose.

Result

The 11 steps map to about 6 real actions, and every application gets the same tracker, resume check, and filing without retyping.

What I would do differently

I'd have mapped the manual steps before building anything. Some steps, like submitting and LinkedIn messages, are better left manual.

How I would start

A proposed plan built around the responsibilities in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet the VeloCFO team and learn how clients work with you today.
  • Map each client's stage, headcount, benefits setup, and open people decisions.
  • Note where a simple process would help most before building anything.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Pick two or three lightweight systems per client, such as an approval path or onboarding checklist.
  • Build a benefits calendar covering renewals, open enrollment, and carrier contacts.
  • Agree a headcount and structure review rhythm with each leadership team.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Roll out the first systems and write short SOPs with named owners.
  • Support a client through open enrollment or a carrier conversation end to end.
  • Review what is working with leaders and adjust.
By month 6

The outcome

  • Clients treat you as a trusted People advisor on team and people decisions.
  • Benefits run smoothly and employee questions get answered quickly.
  • Lightweight systems are in place and improving as each client grows.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the VeloCFO team and clients.
